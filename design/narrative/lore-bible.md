## 1. The Territory at a Glance

Calder Territory is a dry, high, horse-and-cattle territory organised in 1863.
The game opens on **April 3, 1888**, in the third dry spring in a row. The
Calder River — the Territory's spine — is lower than anyone living can
remember, and the Meridian & Western Railroad is racing to have its new line
accepted for a land grant by **June 1888**.

| Region (id) | Character | Economy | Key places |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dustfall Plains (`dustfall`) | Central grassland, cottonwood draws, the Calder valley | Cattle and horse ranching, wheat homesteads | Larkspur, Brannigan Horse Ranch, Halvorsen Homestead, Gallow's Hope, Mustang Flats, Hanging Oak, Eagle Rock |
| Blackwater Basin (`blackwater`) | East; Blackwater Lake, money, rail | Banking, rail freight, lake trade | Port Ashby, Meridian Junction |
| Pine Hollow (`pinehollow`) | North-east forest, Pine Creek | Logging (railroad ties), Sayen horse breeding | Cedar Notch, Sayen Valley Lodge, Veil Falls |
| High Sierra (`sierra`) | North; snow into May | Mountain cattle, trapping | Whitepeak, Hollow Crown Cabin, Cairn Pass |
| The Drylands (`drylands`) | West/SW desert, the Rio Seco dry wash | Silver mining (company town) | Sulfur Flats, Widow Ketterman's Claim, Derailment at Mile 112 |
| Redstone Canyon (`redstone`) | South; mesas, box canyons, the Tinaja River | Army post, outlaws, old ruins | Fort Rourke, Coyle's Roost, Mission San Isidro, Old Cliff Houses, Survey Camp |

**Rivers.** The Calder rises in the High Sierra, runs south past Larkspur and
then east to Blackwater Lake. Pine Creek drains Pine Hollow into the Calder and
marks the eastern end of the Sayen treaty boundary. The Tinaja River cuts through
Redstone. The Rio Seco is a dry wash through the Drylands that runs only after
storms.

**Railroad.** The Meridian & Western main line runs from Port Ashby west to
Meridian Junction (completed 1885). The **Calder Extension** continues west
through Larkspur to Sulfur Flats; it was built in 1887–88 along the *forged*
valley route.

## 3. The Central Scheme (what is actually true)

1. **What the grant gives.** When the Commission accepts the Extension, the
   railroad receives alternate sections along the line plus water-station
   reserves. In the valley, that means **Calder River frontage and its
   riparian water rights.**
2. **The forgery.** The filed survey moves the line from the northern route,
   which takes no ranch land, no river and honours the 1886 Sayen agreement, to
   the valley route. Under the forged plats, nine ranch and homestead claims
   appear to conflict with the grant.
3. **The squeeze.** The Townsite Company (secretly Vance's) offers about $4 an
   acre and hints that a claim may be worth nothing "under the revised survey".
   Coyle's rustling strips ranches of the horses they need to work cattle.
   Owners miss their bank notes, and they sell.
4. **The profit.** Vance's company buys river frontage cheap now and holds an
   option on the railroad's grant sections. In a drought year, whoever holds the
   Calder controls the valley.
5. **The company.** The Meridian & Western board did not order the forgery, but
   it benefited, asked no questions, and will disown Vance as "a former employee
   acting wholly on his own account".

**Evidence chain:** the scorched requisition (`wagon_manifest`) → the Fort
Rourke bill of sale (`remount_bill`) → Kessler's telegram copy (optional) →
Pruitt's field book (`pruitt_notebook`) → Silas's affidavit
(`silas_affidavit`). Pruitt's book plus the affidavit are the legally decisive
pair.

## 5. Regional Cultures

- **Larkspur.** A cow town of about 400 at the Calder ford. Its people are
  practical and gossipy, and they keep accounts. The Gilded Spur is its living
  room, Pettibone's ledger its memory and Josiah Oakes's livery its heart. The
  town is mixed: Black, Mexican, Anglo, Cornish, and a Nigerian-born doctor
  trained in Edinburgh. Nobody comments on this more than 1888 people would. The
  town just functions.
- **Port Ashby.** A lake city of about 3,000 with brick fronts, electric bells
  at the Lakeshore Hotel, a daily newspaper, a bank and a Territorial Marshal.
  Railroad money drinks at The Lakeview. Water Street is where the people who
  serve it live.
- **The Calder valley ranches.** Family operations that run horses as much as
  cattle. They share water by custom and are suspicious of paper.
- **Sulfur Flats.** A company town. Everyone is paid in scrip that is good only
  at the Company Store. Its crews are Welsh, Cornish, Mexican and Chinese, and
  its saloon is honest about its whiskey.
- **Cedar Notch.** A seasonal logging camp cutting ties for the railroad that is
  eating its forest. It is mostly Scandinavian, and Marta Aune runs it.
- **Whitepeak.** Mountain cattle and a trading post. Snowbound five months a
  year, and therefore left alone by speculators.
- **Fort Rourke.** Two understrength cavalry troops. Black veterans like Sgt.
  Fry serve alongside green West Pointers.
- **Sayen Valley.** A contemporary, self-governing nation of horse breeders and
  ranchers living in log and frame houses around the Council House and lodge.
  They are literate in English and their own language, and they sell horses to
  the Army and Eastern buyers. The council has internal politics: Mareh
  (patient, institutional), Isaac (legal, ambitious, pro-agreement), Tolani
  (commercial and pragmatic) and Corin (impatient, wants direct action).

## 6. Economy Notes (1888 prices, for consistency)

- Ranch land under duress: Townsite Company offers **$4/acre**. Fair value with
  water is $10–15.
- An Army remount sells for up to **$95/head**. Sound Sayen Appaloosas bring
  **$140** in Port Ashby.
- Ranch hand $30/month and found. Logger $1/day. Miner $2.50/day in scrip,
  worth about 70¢ on the dollar at the Company Store.
- Bounties: Rattler Pike $200, Dutch Coyle $350.
- Vance's hush money: $500, which is more than a ranch hand earns in a year.

## 7. Consistency Rules

1. **No supernatural.** Legends such as the Pale Mare and the Cliff Houses have
   mundane explanations or are left open as stories. Sayen characters are never
   mystical.
2. **Sayen portrayal.** They are a contemporary 1880s nation with agency, money,
   law, internal disagreement and modern concerns: water, rail, fraud and
   schooling. They speak fluent, individual English, never "broken" English. No
   tipis, war paint, headdresses or "noble savage" framing. Their names are
   invented, or English.
3. **Not a revenge story.** Silas survives. The protagonist's motive is
   protecting family and a valley, not vengeance. Dutch Coyle comes to the
   protagonist, not the other way around.
4. **The railroad is not evil; men are.** The railroad as a road is neutral, and
   even good: Tolani wants the siding. The villainy is Vance's forgery and the
   company's convenient blindness.
5. **The protagonist** is a former Army remount wrangler of unspecified name and
   gender, Silas's kin, and first-person in journals. NPCs say "Brannigan's kin",
   "partner", "friend" and "stranger". Never "nephew", "niece", "son", "sir" or
   "ma'am" directed at the player.
6. **Saloon "hostesses"** exist off-screen at The Lakeview, implied only.
7. **Dates.** Arrival is April 3. The fight at the wagon is March 29 and the
   shooting at the Roost is March 30. The grant acceptance is due in June. Don't
   contradict these.
8. **Geography.** The Brannigan ranch is south-west of Larkspur. The burned wagon
   is a couple of miles west of the ranch. Fort Rourke is south, and the Roost
   is south-east of the fort. The mission and survey camp are far south-west.
   Hollow Crown is west of Whitepeak.
9. **Endings.** Three canonical end-states: `ending_law`, `ending_press` and
   `ending_bribe`. Any content written after the main story must check them.
